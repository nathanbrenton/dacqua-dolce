# D'Acqua Dolce — Audience Copy Lexicon

**Purpose:** A reusable language bank for public-site copy, product storytelling, editorial content, CTAs, and supporting microcopy.

**Companion to:** `DESIGN_RESEARCH_20260905.md` (research reference supplied separately)

This lexicon translates the research's three strongest audience-language modes into a practical copywriting reference:

- **Performance** — precise, capable, efficient, measurable, dependable.
- **Culinary** — ingredient-focused, sensory, crafted, editorial.
- **Wellness** — intentional, restorative, balanced, lifestyle-oriented.

Words may intentionally repeat across categories. Shared vocabulary is useful because D'Acqua Dolce should feel like one brand rather than three unrelated brands.

## Usage principles

1. **Pepper, don't saturate.** One or two audience-signaling words in a section are usually stronger than stacking buzzwords.
2. **Match language to context.** Performance language fits specifications, comparisons, maintenance, and system behavior. Culinary language fits storytelling, kitchens, taste, preparation, and hospitality. Wellness language fits brand, lifestyle, ownership, and emotional framing.
3. **Keep technical claims separate from aesthetic language.** Evocative words can establish tone, but measurable performance, health, contaminant reduction, capacity, certification, or purity claims require authoritative support.
4. **Avoid audience lock-in.** A page can signal one audience without implying that every customer belongs to that demographic.
5. **Prefer concrete nouns and verbs over dense adjective stacks.**

## Copy-direction note: “goals”

“Goals” naturally leans toward the **performance / optimization** audience. It remains useful, but repeated use can make the brand sound fitness-oriented.

To vary audience signals, rotate nearby language such as:

- **Performance:** goals, performance, precision, consistency, readiness
- **Culinary:** taste, craft, preparation, ingredient, clarity
- **Wellness:** ritual, balance, lifestyle, everyday, wellbeing
- **Neutral bridges:** priorities, preferences, approach, experience, what matters most

## Consultation-language examples

Friendly, question-based prompts that avoid commands such as “Tell us…”:

- How can we help you filter water?
- How can we help with your water?
- What matters most about your water?
- What would you like from your water?
- What could make your water experience better?
- What are you looking for in your water?
- Where would you like to begin with water treatment?

`How can we help you filter water?` is warm and direct, but **filter** is narrower than the full D'Acqua Dolce treatment portfolio. It works well where filtration is genuinely the subject; broader consultation surfaces may benefit from `How can we help with your water?`.
